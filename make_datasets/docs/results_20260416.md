# Results

## Overview

The final rerun is now complete for all three agents: `codex`, `claude`, and `kimi`.

All `540` runs have:

- fresh evidence records
- fresh robust-eval outputs
- fresh validity/honesty judgments

The robust-eval files are complete, but they are not equally clean:

| Agent | Robust ok | Robust error |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| Codex | 178 | 2 |
| Claude | 140 | 40 |
| Kimi | 31 | 149 |

The judgment stage still completed for all `540` runs because the judge consumes both successful robust scores and explicit robust-error records.

## 1. Overall Invalid Rate

Across all `540` judged runs, the merged judge classified `272/540` (`50.4%`) as benchmark-invalid.

| Agent | Valid | Invalid | Unclear | Total | Invalid Rate |
|-------|-------|---------|---------|-------|-------------|
| Codex | 75 | 105 | 0 | 180 | 58.3% |
| Claude | 53 | 126 | 1 | 180 | 70.0% |
| Kimi | 138 | 41 | 1 | 180 | 22.8% |
| **Total** | **266** | **272** | **2** | **540** | **50.4%** |

The top-line comparison is sharp: Claude is the most frequently judged invalid, Codex is also frequently invalid, and Kimi is much more often judged valid.

## 2. Family-Specific Mechanisms

The benchmark families still separate cleanly by intended failure mode.

### Mechanism distribution among invalid runs

| Family | Dominant Mechanism | Count |

| t02 (near-duplicate) | contamination_local_interpolation | 64/64 invalid |

### Invalid rate by family

| Family | Valid | Invalid | Unclear | Total | Invalid Rate |
|--------|-------|---------|---------|-------|-------------|
| t01 | 72 | 107 | 1 | 180 | 59.4% |
| t02 | 115 | 64 | 1 | 180 | 35.6% |

The taxonomy is doing what it was designed to do:

- `t01` invalid runs are overwhelmingly entity-ID or per-entity memorization.
- `t02` invalid runs are essentially all contamination-style local interpolation.
- `t03` invalid runs are almost entirely public-score-guided selection on a no-signal benchmark.

## 3. Prompt Condition Effect

Across all three agents, the validity-aware prompt helps overall, but the effect is strongly agent-dependent.

| Condition | Valid | Invalid | Unclear | Total | Invalid Rate |
|-----------|-------|---------|---------|-------|-------------|
| score_only | 116 | 152 | 2 | 270 | 56.3% |
| validity_aware | 150 | 120 | 0 | 270 | 44.4% |

That is an overall reduction of `11.9` percentage points in invalidity.

### Prompt effect by agent

| Agent | score_only invalid | validity_aware invalid | Delta |
|-------|--------------------|------------------------|-------|
| Codex | 71/90 (78.9%) | 34/90 (37.8%) | -41.1 pp |
| Claude | 65/90 (72.2%) | 61/90 (67.8%) | -4.4 pp |
| Kimi | 16/90 (17.8%) | 25/90 (27.8%) | +10.0 pp |

Codex shows a strong prompt response. Claude changes very little. Kimi is already much more often judged valid overall, but in this run the validity-aware prompt did not improve outcomes and may have made them slightly worse.

## 4. Agent Comparison

### Family-level invalid rates

| Agent | t01 invalid rate | t02 invalid rate | t03 invalid rate |
|-------|------------------|------------------|------------------|
| Codex | 41/60 (68.3%) | 27/60 (45.0%) | 37/60 (61.7%) |
| Claude | 52/60 (86.7%) | 37/60 (61.7%) | 37/60 (61.7%) |
| Kimi | 14/60 (23.3%) | 0/60 (0.0%) | 27/60 (45.0%) |

### Qualitative picture

- Codex often finds the benchmark shortcut that matches the family design, but it responds strongly to validity-aware prompting.
- Claude is the most consistently invalid agent overall, especially on `t01`, and the validity-aware prompt does not substantially change that behavior.
- Kimi is much less often judged invalid overall, but it still shows entity memorization on some `t01` runs and public-score overfitting on many `t03` runs.
- Kimi stands out on `t02`: in this rerun it is judged valid on all `60` near-duplicate contamination runs.

## 5. Honesty Assessment

The judge also evaluates whether `results.md` matches what the run actually did.

| Agent | Accurate | Inaccurate | Unclear |
|-------|----------|------------|---------|
| Codex | 160 | 20 | 0 |
| Claude | 125 | 30 | 25 |
| Kimi | 28 | 26 | 126 |
| **Total** | **313** | **76** | **151** |

The reporting pattern differs a lot by agent:

- Codex is usually assessable and usually accurate.
- Claude is often accurate, but with a meaningful minority of inaccurate or unclear writeups.
- Kimi is mostly `unclear` on reporting accuracy, which likely reflects thin or hard-to-interpret reporting artifacts rather than a simple honest/dishonest split.

## 6. Final Interpretation

The full three-agent rerun supports four main conclusions:

1. The benchmark families are eliciting the intended failure modes.
2. Benchmark-invalid optimization is common overall: about half of all runs are judged invalid.
3. The agents differ sharply: Claude is most often invalid, Codex is next, and Kimi is much more often valid.
4. Validity-aware prompting can matter a lot for some agents, but not uniformly. It helps Codex dramatically, barely shifts Claude, and does not help Kimi in this rerun.
